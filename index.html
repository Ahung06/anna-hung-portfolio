Unified Engineering Flight Competition

May 2026

The Unified Engineering Flight Competition (UEFC) is one of the final projects of MIT's Unified Engineering courses. Teams design and build RC aircraft optimized for a complex scoring metric combining speed flight performance with payload positioning. Our team developed an optimizer to identify promising values for aspect ratio, wing area, and velocity, then validated the design using vortex lattice analysis. We prioritized a robust, predictable aircraft design over an aggressive design, ensuring reliable performance while remaining competitive.

Design & Results:

  • Developed custom optimizer for aspect ratio, wing area, and velocity
  • Validated design using vortex lattice analysis
  • Analyzed spiral stability and selected 8° dihedral angle for stable recovery
  • Resolved initial wing area constraint violation by increasing wing area with safety margin
  • Maintained acceptable induced drag and angle of attack
  • First place in speed flight; Second place overall

JetJoe Gas Turbine Engine Lab

April 2026

The JetJoe Lab is one of the final projects of MIT's Unified Engineering courses, covering modeling and analysis, engine assembly, and operation of a small-scale gas turbine jet engine. We used the JetJoe JJ-1400 model jet engine kit and worked in teams to establish an aero-thermodynamic performance model of the jet engine and estimate performance based on geometry information and component performance assumptions. The jet engine was then tested on an experimental test stand to assess real performance, validating our thermodynamic predictions.

Key Contributions:

  • Formulated thermodynamic cycle model using Unified Engineering concepts (16.003 and 16.004)
  • Conducted mean-line through-flow analysis using component performance estimates
  • Extracted critical geometric and aero-thermodynamic parameters from jet engine hardware
  • Derived gas turbine cycle parameters to match specified EGT, SFC, and maximum thrust
  • Created performance comparison tables between predicted and experimental results
  • Assembled and disassembled model jet engine with precision instrumentation
  • Installed engine in GTL small engine test-stand and conducted full system testing
  • Analyzed measurement uncertainties and their impact on cycle parameter estimates
  • Best performing team of the day; Third place in cycle parameter estimation

MIT Nuclear: Hydrogen Test Facility

April 2026

The Shirvan Group is developing a system to test Nuclear Thermal Propulsion (NTP) fuels, which are critical for advancing the future of spaceflight. NTP systems offer a more efficient propulsion mechanism by using a nuclear reactor to heat a lightweight propellant like hydrogen instead of relying on chemical combustion. This process greatly increases the specific impulse of conventional rockets. This project involves designing, constructing, and testing a hydrogen gas system that will be installed in the MIT Reactor. The work combines hands-on engineering with system-level testing, acting as a practical learning opportunity to support the development of next-generation nuclear propulsion technology for space exploration.

Key Contributions:

  • Designed hydrogen gas system for NTP fuel testing within reactor constraints
  • Developed SolidWorks 3D assembly with comprehensive pipe routing for hydrogen lines
  • Designed fluid system architecture with pressure regulators, isolation valves, and flow control
  • Integrated hydrogen leak detection system for safety monitoring
  • Engineered system components for reliable operation at elevated temperatures
  • Coordinated system layout considering limited footprint on MIT Reactor
  • Planned system-level testing protocols for hydrogen safety and performance validation
  • Supporting development of advanced nuclear thermal propulsion for next-generation spacecraft

MIT Rocket Team: Project Aurora (Aerodynamics)

May 2025

Project Aurora is a 10-foot-tall, single-stage rocket developed by the MIT Rocket Team to compete in the Spaceport America Cup, aiming for a 30,000-foot altitude (recently achieving ~14,000 ft). The project features in-house designed composite fins, reefing parachutes, and a SRAD (Student-Researched And Designed) solid rocket motor. The aerodynamics team focused on comprehensive analysis including fin can assembly, stability analysis, load testing, flutter analysis, and manufacturing optimization.

Aerodynamics Contributions:

  • Conducted CFD analysis using SolidWorks Flow Simulation at Mach 1 and Mach 2
  • Designed and optimized camera shell aerodynamics
  • Designed fin can assembly with integration for rocket body
  • Developed fin can testing mount for structural validation
  • Optimized fin leading edge for shock wave management and aerodynamic efficiency
  • Analyzed fin manufacturing requirements and tolerances
  • Conducted flutter analysis and stability verification
  • Coordinated aerodynamic load cases with structural team
